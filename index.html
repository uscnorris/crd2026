Poster session

The afternoon poster session is open to everyone at the Cancer Center: faculty, staff, trainees, and research centers, across every program.

Submitting a poster

Registration and poster submission use the same form, so one submission covers both.

Where posters are set up

Posters are grouped by topic around Pappas Quad. Your section letter (the letter before the dash in your poster number) tells you which side to look on.

Schematic only, not to scale. Board numbers are posted on-site.

Awards

The poster session is open to all. The judged competition for awards is open to trainees only (i.e., students, postdoctoral fellows, clinical fellows, residents).

Three prizes of $100

All trainees presenting posters are automatically eligible for the judged competition.

What judges look for

  • Scientific merit. The significance of the question and the soundness of the approach.
  • Clarity of presentation. How well the poster and the presenter communicate the work.
  • Originality. What is new about the approach or the findings.
  • Overall impression. The judges' holistic view of the poster and the conversation.

First time at a poster session?

1
Walk the rows. Dozens of posters line Pappas Quad. Browse in any order.
2
Talk to presenters. Say hi and ask for the two-minute version. It is a completely normal thing to ask for.
3
Ask questions. "What surprised you?" or "What's next?" go a long way. No expertise required.
4
Use the app. Browse the directory to see who's presenting and what they're working on.

For trainees

Students, postdoctoral fellows, and clinical fellows can take part in three ways, not just attend.

1

Present a poster

Open to everyone at the Center, but only trainees are eligible for the three $100 prizes. Submit when you register.

Poster details and awards →
2

Give a talk

Trainee speakers may be featured in the morning session and on the AI panel. Nominate yourself or a colleague through your PI or program leader, or email CRTEC at crtec@usc.edu.

3

Coffee Consult

New for 2026. One conversation across the bench-to-bedside divide, arranged for you.

Who it pairs
One research trainee (PhD student or postdoctoral fellow) with one clinical trainee (clinical fellow, resident, or clinical doctoral student).
What you talk about
The clinical reasoning behind the science: what a finding would mean at the bedside, and what questions the clinic actually needs answered.
How you get matched
Open the Coffee Consult tab in the directory, find someone on the other side, and request a match by 5 PM the next day, Oct 15. CRTEC arranges every match, so nobody is cold-contacting anyone.
What happens next
CRTEC confirms your match and emails you both to arrange a time. Coffee is on us.
